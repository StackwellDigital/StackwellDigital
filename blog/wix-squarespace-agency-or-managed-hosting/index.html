Wix, Squarespace, an Agency or Managed Hosting? An Honest Comparison

By Adam VolletOctober 26, 20263 min read

I run a managed hosting business, so you can guess which way I lean. But I've also been a customer of the other options, and I don't think any of them is a scam. They just suit different people. Here's how I'd think about it if I were choosing for my own business.

Option one: DIY builders like Wix, Squarespace and GoDaddy

You pick a template, drag things around and publish. Setup can be quick and the monthly cost is often low.

What's good:

  • You can get online in a weekend.
  • Low upfront cost.
  • Everything lives in one dashboard.

What to watch for:

  • You're the designer, writer, photographer and tech support. Most owners underestimate how much time that takes.
  • Prices change, and add ons for things like booking, extra email or removing platform branding can pile up.
  • Templates make lots of businesses look alike, and performance and SEO control can be limited.
  • Moving away later can be a pain. Your content doesn't always export cleanly.

Best for: someone with spare time, a simple need, and the patience to learn a platform.

Option two: a traditional agency

A team designs and builds a custom site for you. It's the full service route.

What's good:

  • Professional design and strategy.
  • Specialists for branding, copy and development.
  • Good for larger or more complex projects.

What to watch for:

  • Often a large upfront build fee, and then ongoing retainer fees for care and updates.
  • Slow turnarounds on small changes, sometimes billed by the hour.
  • Be sure you know who owns the domain and the site files when the project ends.

Best for: bigger organizations or businesses with complex needs and a budget to match.

Option three: a freelancer

One person builds your site, often at a friendlier price than an agency.

What's good:

  • Direct contact with the person doing the work.
  • Flexible and often affordable.

What to watch for:

  • Quality varies a lot, so ask to see real past work.
  • If they get busy or move on, who maintains your site? Who has the passwords?
  • Hosting, security and updates may not be part of the deal unless you ask.

Best for: a defined one time project, as long as you own everything at the end and have a plan for upkeep.

Option four: managed hosting with a build included

This is where we sit. A provider builds the site, then hosts and looks after it for a flat monthly fee. You don't deal with servers, updates or security, and you can ask for changes when you need them.

What's good:

  • One predictable monthly price instead of a big lump sum.
  • Someone else handles hosting, security, backups and updates.
  • Changes are included, so you don't wait for a quote every time you need a tweak.

What to watch for:

  • You're relying on the provider to stay around, so check how easy it is to leave. Ask about the domain and your content.
  • Not every provider includes the same things. Read what the fee actually covers.
  • It isn't the right fit if you want to tinker with every pixel yourself.

Best for: busy owners who want a good looking site that stays working without becoming a second job.

Questions worth asking any provider

  • Who owns the domain, and can I log in to that account?
  • What exactly does the monthly fee include?
  • If I leave, what happens to my site and my content?
  • Is there a contract, and what does it cost to cancel?
  • Who handles security, backups and certificates?
  • How fast do you respond when something breaks?

A good provider will answer these without squirming. If you get vague answers, that tells you something.
